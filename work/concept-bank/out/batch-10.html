☆ Deep Ocean Mission and Samudrayaan

Why it exists

Exploration of deep ocean resources, mineral nodule beds, and benthic biodiversity requires indigenous manned submersibles and underwater robotics.

How it works

National ocean technology institutes engineer titanium pressure hulls and deep-sea mining crawlers, conducting phased trials in international abyssal waters.

Fixed facts

  • Part of Deep Ocean Mission; Matsya submersible (MATSYA-6000) under Samudrayaan → 3 aquanauts; operating depth 5,000–6,000 m
  • Deep Ocean Mission (MoES): approved by CCEA 16 Jun 2021, ₹4,077 crore for 5 years

Traps

Deep Ocean Mission focuses on exploration and sustainable extraction of oceanic resources rather than surface commercial merchant shipping.

In the news

  • First human deep-sea mission to 6,000 m by 2028
☆ IndiaAI Mission and compute infrastructure

Why it exists

Developing sovereign artificial intelligence capacity and indigenous large language models requires subsidised high-performance GPU supercomputing infrastructure.

How it works

The technology ministry subsidizes access to graphic processor compute capacity, funds open-source foundational datasets, and incubates domestic machine learning startups.

Fixed facts

  • Launched in New Delhi (MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan) under the IndiaAI Mission (MeitY); teams chosen under the Foundation Models pillar of the IndiaAI Innovation Centre
  • BharatGen: sovereign multilingual LLM (IIT Bombay-led; ₹235 crore NM-ICPS); India AI Mission outlay ₹10,585 crore; BHASHINI: 22 voice languages; VoicERA open-source voice AI stack

Traps

The mission focuses on foundational compute access and sovereign language models rather than individual commercial software licensing.

In the news

  • IndiaAI Mission: foundation models EKA-1, Dhir 2B and Dhir 5B launched
  • MANAV Vision for AI (India AI Impact Summit 2026)
☆ National Quantum Mission

Why it exists

Advances in quantum computing and communications threaten classical cryptographic security, requiring indigenous quantum hardware and secure key distribution.

How it works

The department of science establishes specialized thematic technology hubs across premier institutes to develop superconducting qubits, optical sensors, and quantum links.

Fixed facts

  • NQM : Cabinet 19 Apr 2023; ₹ 6,003.65 cr (2023-24 to 2030-31); DST. 1,000 km quantum-secure communication achieved in 3 years (8-year target 2,000 km). Targets 50–1,000 qubit computers.
  • 4 Thematic Hubs: Computing – IISc Bengaluru; Communication – IIT Madras (+ C-DoT); Sensing and Metrology – IIT Bombay; Materials and Devices – IIT Delhi.

Traps

Quantum key distribution provides cryptographic physics-based transmission security rather than building consumer desktop computers.

In the news

  • National Quantum Mission and quantum links
  • National Quantum Mission: 1,000 km secure link
☆ National Supercomputing Mission

Why it exists

National computational research in drug discovery, climate modelling, and nuclear physics demands networked domestic petascale supercomputing systems.

How it works

Central engineering bodies deploy indigenous computing clusters connected across high-speed research grids to provide compute cycles to universities.

Fixed facts

  • 40 supercomputers, 68 PF (Sep 2026); target 50 systems, above 123 PF. Launched Apr 2015, outlay about ₹4,500 cr; DST + MeitY; implemented by C-DAC Pune and IISc Bengaluru.
  • PARAM Rudra series (indigenous Rudra servers by C-DAC); PARAM Shavak (supercomputing-in-a-box). Backbone: National Knowledge Network. Static: PARAM 8000 (1991).

Traps

The mission deploys institutional petascale research clusters rather than commercial internet cloud web hosting services.

In the news

  • NSM
☆ ISRO earth observation and remote sensing missions

Why it exists

Accurate agriculture yield estimation, disaster flood mapping, and territorial surveillance require multispectral satellite imagery in diverse orbital paths.

How it works

National launch vehicles place thermal, radar, and optical imaging payloads into polar and geosynchronous orbits to transmit continuous radiometric data.

Fixed facts

  • India’s first imaging satellite in geosynchronous orbit (sub-geosynchronous), from Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota
  • Real-time Earth imagery for agriculture and disaster management

Traps

Earth observation satellites capture optical and synthetic aperture surface data rather than serving as transponder relay stations for television broadcast.

In the news

  • ISRO launches EOS-05 on GSLV-F17
  • TRISHNA: ISRO–CNES thermal imaging mission
  • GSLV-F18 launches NVS-03 navigation satellite
☆ Agnipath scheme for military recruitment

Why it exists

Modern armed forces require a youthful demographic profile and technological adaptability while managing burgeoning long-term pension expenditure.

How it works

Recruits undertake four-year military contracts with specialized training, after which a merit fraction transitions to permanent regular military commissions.

Fixed facts

  • First 4 years → 25% offered regular service on merit
  • Remaining 75% → optional extra 2 years = Second Tour of Duty

Traps

Enrolment under the scheme provides fixed-term operational service rather than granting immediate permanent lifelong military commission benefits.

In the news

  • Cabinet approves 2-year "Second Tour of Duty" for Agniveers
☆ Defence Acquisition Council and indigenisation

Why it exists

Strategic defense autonomy and supply security require domestic manufacturing of weapon platforms instead of perpetual reliance on imported foreign systems.

How it works

The apex ministerial council grants capital project approvals and positive indigenisation lists, mandating domestic research and manufacturing contracts.

Fixed facts

  • Defence Acquisition Council (chair Rajnath Singh): AoN (Acceptance of Necessity = first-stage approval) for ~ ₹1.10 lakh cr ; ~ 98% from Indian industry
  • GE Aerospace delivers 3 more F404-IN20 engines for Tejas Mk1A; HAL is the 14th Maharatna CPSE

Traps

Acceptance of Necessity represents the preliminary procurement sanction rather than the final signature of commercial manufacturing contracts.

  • HAL: F404-IN20 engines, Maharatna status, deliveries
  • India-Russia RELOS pact
☆ CAR-T cell immunotherapy

Why it exists

Targeted cellular immunotherapy provides treatment options for patients with refractory blood cancers when conventional chemotherapy and radiation therapies fail to achieve remission.

How it works

Patient T cells are genetically modified with chimeric antigen receptors to recognise cancer surface proteins, multiplied in specialised laboratory facilities, and reinfused into the patient.

Fixed facts

  • CAR-T = chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy (immunotherapy using patient's own cells); for B-cell cancers (leukaemia, lymphoma).
  • Built by IIT Bombay + Tata Memorial Centre + ImmunoACT ; first CAR-T approved by CDSCO (Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation).

Traps

CAR-T therapy is an autologous treatment using the patient's own engineered cells rather than an off-the-shelf donor blood transfusion.

In the news

  • NexCAR19: India's first indigenous CAR-T therapy
☆ Human Papillomavirus vaccine and cancer prevention

Why it exists

Vaccination prevents persistent human papillomavirus infection, which causes cervical cancer and related malignancies in women.

How it works

Quadrivalent vaccines generate antibodies against specific viral capsid proteins, conferring immunity before initial exposure to high-risk strains.

Fixed facts

  • Gender-neutral; licensed in India for ages 9–26; prevents cervical cancer and other HPV-related disease.
  • PM Modi launched national HPV programme for girls aged 14 from Ajmer ; free at government facilities ("Swastha Nari")

Traps

The national immunisation programme targets specific age cohorts before exposure, while private licensing extends across a wider age bracket.

In the news

  • CERVAVAC: first Indian HPV vaccine WHO-prequalified
  • HPV vaccination drive launched from Ajmer (Rajasthan)
☆ Vehicular emission norms and clean air actions

Why it exists

Controlling vehicular tailpipe emissions and airborne particulate matter is necessary to reduce toxic air pollution in dense urban regions.

How it works

Stricter vehicle construction rules mandate advanced exhaust filters, particulate monitoring thresholds, and progressive fleet modernisation incentives.

Fixed facts

  • Draft notification by the Road Transport and Highways Ministry amending the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989; aligned with Europe's Euro 7.
  • Phased from April 2029 to April 2035: cars and SUVs (category M1) April 2029; small goods vehicles and pick-ups 2030; buses and heavy vehicles April 2031; two-wheelers to be notified separately.

Traps

Bharat Stage emission standards regulate vehicle manufacturing specifications rather than ambient atmospheric weather conditions.

In the news

  • Draft Bharat Stage VII (BS-VII) emission norms
  • PARIVARTAN: old truck and bus replacement scheme (Delhi-NCR)
  • India’s Path to Blue Skies: clean air facts
☆ Indigenous defence drones and UAVs

Why it exists

Modern electronic warfare and border security require indigenous unmanned aerial platforms for surveillance, precision strike, and counter-drone defence.

How it works

Armed forces deploy multi-tiered drone systems comprising loitering attack munitions, airborne interceptors, and tactical detection battalions.

Fixed facts

  • Platforms: KAL (one-way attack drone, up to 1,000 km); BAJ (drone interceptor); JWALA (counter-drone micro-missile, in development)
  • First Baaz Battalion (drone unit) at Jalandhar, 11 Corps; post-Operation Sindoor; operates MQ-9B, Heron etc.

Traps

Loitering attack munitions operate as expendable aerial weapons rather than reusable transport surveillance drones.

In the news

  • KAL: India’s first indigenous long-range attack drone tested at Pokhran
☆ Solid Waste Management and EPR rules

Why it exists

Mitigating urban landfill accumulation and environmental degradation requires statutory waste segregation and manufacturer accountability for packaging waste.

How it works

Municipal authorities mandate household waste separation into distinct streams while producers face statutory recycling and resource recovery quotas.

Fixed facts

  • Solid Waste Management Rules 2026 replace 2016 rules: four-way segregation (wet, dry, sanitary, special care); RDF substitution 5% → 15% over six years; polluter pays
  • Based on circular economy and Extended Producer Responsibility ; mandatory four-stream segregation: wet, dry, sanitary, special care

Traps

Extended Producer Responsibility mandates recycling obligations for packaging producers rather than shifting processing costs onto consumers.

In the news

  • Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026
☆ Great Indian Bustard conservation framework

Why it exists

Severe habitat loss and power line collisions have reduced bustard populations to critical levels, necessitating strict legal and in-situ protection.

How it works

Conservation authorities demarcate protected desert grassland reserves, implement underground power-transmission cabling, and establish captive breeding centres.

Fixed facts

  • Ardeotis nigriceps; mainly Rajasthan’s Thar Desert (small numbers in Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh)
  • IUCN Critically Endangered ; CITES Appendix I ; CMS Appendix I; Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 Schedule I

Traps

State protection concentrates in western desert grasslands though historical distribution spanned across multiple central and peninsular Indian states.

In the news

  • Great Indian Bustard: protection status
