★ Renewable energy capacity and green corridors

In plain words: National policies expand clean energy installations across solar and wind sectors to shift power generation toward non-fossil sources.

Why it exists

Surging national power consumption and international climate pledges require expanding domestic renewable capacity to reduce thermal coal dependence.

How it works

Designated renewable generation zones feed high-voltage transmission lines, while grid utilities procure wind and solar power through transparent competitive auctions.

Fixed facts

  • Target 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; installed 308.027 GW (Sept 2026); added about 55.29 GW in FY 2025-26
  • Added in 2025–26: over 55 GW; target 500 GW by 2030; solar and wind lead.

Traps

Intermittent solar and wind power generation requires balancing capacity from hydro storage or grid battery installations rather than running base load without firming.

In the news

  • India crosses 300 GW non-fossil capacity
★ UNFCCC Conference of Parties and Paris commitments

In plain words: Parties to the international climate framework convention negotiate binding commitments and voluntary pledges to reduce global atmospheric emissions.

Why it exists

Rising global average temperatures trigger catastrophic environmental disruption, requiring coordinated multilateral mitigation goals and financing for vulnerable nations.

How it works

National governments submit periodic climate contribution pledges, while annual multilateral conferences review compliance and coordinate climate finance flows.

Fixed facts

  • COP31: Antalya, Turkiye, 9–20 Nov 2026; priorities set out by Bhupender Yadav
  • Seek decision to start a Climate Finance Work Programme → finance as obligation of developed countries (Article 9.1); predictable, mostly public, concessional, not debt-creating

Traps

National emission reduction contributions remain self-determined by sovereign governments rather than being imposed through punitive international sanctions.

In the news

  • COP31: India's stand
  • COP31 (Conference of the Parties)
  • NDC Synthesis Report 2026 and adaptation finance
  • India's NDC 2031-35
★ International Solar Alliance

In plain words: An intergovernmental coalition coordinates clean technology adoption and capital mobilization for solar power projects across member countries.

Why it exists

Tropical and developing countries face high financing costs and supply chain constraints despite having substantial year-round solar irradiation.

How it works

The treaty-based alliance pools solar project procurement, coordinates multilateral concessional finance, and conducts technical training for national power engineers.

Fixed facts

  • France re-elected Co-President for 2027–2029 (defeated Burkina Faso); €50,000 voluntary contribution for 2026
  • ISA: founded by India and France, 2015; Secretariat Surya Bhawan, Gurugram; 114 members (signed and ratified) + 16 signatories yet to ratify

Traps

The alliance is an independent treaty-based international organization headquartered in India rather than a specialized agency of the United Nations.

In the news

  • International Solar Alliance: India re-elected President (3rd term)
★ Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule)

In plain words: A constitutional schedule disqualifies elected legislative members who defect from their political party or defy binding party floor directives.

Why it exists

Corrupt floor-crossing and opportunistic party switching destabilize elected parliamentary governments and violate democratic voter mandates.

How it works

The legislative presiding officer examines petitions alleging unlawful party defection and issues binding disqualification rulings under constitutional procedures.

Fixed facts

  • Added by the 52nd Amendment Act 1985; the 91st Amendment removed the one-third split provision, now two-thirds merger needed
  • Tenth Schedule: 52nd Amendment 1985; 91st Amendment: two-thirds merger needed

Traps

Disqualification decisions are delivered by the speaker or chairman of the house subject to judicial review, rather than automatic removal by the election commission.

In the news

  • Tenth Schedule (anti-defection)
★ Parliamentary delimitation and legislative seat allocation

In plain words: Statutory commissions readjust legislative territorial constituencies and allocate parliamentary seats among states according to updated census demographics.

Why it exists

Unequal population growth across administrative regions produces disproportionate voter representation without periodic constitutional reapportionment of legislative seats.

How it works

An independent delimitation commission reviews official census data, draws balanced constituency territorial boundaries, and reserves specific seats for disadvantaged communities.

Fixed facts

  • Lok Sabha 543 → up to 850: not more than 815 from states, up to 35 from UTs (Article 81); Articles 82 and 170 amended for Delimitation Commission allocation on updated census
  • New Article 334A: women's reservation in Lok Sabha and assemblies, comes into force after delimitation; lasts 15 years unless extended

Traps

Constituency boundaries redrawn by a statutory delimitation commission have the force of law and cannot be questioned in any court.

In the news

  • Constitutional Amendment Bill: Lok Sabha up to 850 seats
★ Panchayats Extension to Scheduled Areas (PESA) Act

In plain words: A national statute extends local self-government institutions to designated tribal areas while securing customary rights over land, water, and forests.

Why it exists

Standard panchayati raj structures lacked safeguards for indigenous customary governance, leading to land alienation and natural resource exploitation in tribal habitations.

How it works

Village assemblies in scheduled areas exercise mandatory consultation powers regarding land acquisition, minor forest produce ownership, and local dispute settlement.

Fixed facts

  • PESA Act 1996 covers Fifth Schedule areas of 10 states incl. Rajasthan (77,564 villages, 22,040 Panchayats, 664 blocks); Centre of Excellence at IGNTU Amarkantak; Odisha is the only state yet to notify PESA rules
  • Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act enacted 24 Dec 1996 for Fifth Schedule (tribal) areas; inspired by 73rd and 74th Amendments

Traps

Gram sabha authority in scheduled areas is autonomous and customary, meaning higher administrative bodies cannot override village consent for forest concessions.

In the news

  • PESA Act rules in Jharkhand
★ Armed Forces Special Powers Act

In plain words: A special statute empowers federal armed forces operating in declared disturbed areas to maintain public order with broad search and arrest powers.

Why it exists

Severe separatist insurgencies and cross-border militant violence exceed the operational capability of local civilian police forces to maintain public order.

How it works

The central or state government notifies specific districts as disturbed areas, granting armed personnel immunity from prosecution without prior central sanction.

Fixed facts

  • Who: Ministry of Home Affairs; Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958; 6 months from 1 Oct 2026; 'disturbed areas' notified under Section 3.
  • Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958 extended in certain areas of Manipur, Nagaland and Arunachal Pradesh

Traps

Armed forces personnel cannot be prosecuted in civilian courts for official acts without prior mandatory statutory prosecution sanction from the central government.

In the news

  • AFSPA extended in three states
★ Shanghai Cooperation Organisation

In plain words: A regional intergovernmental organization coordinates political cooperation, security alignment, trade logistics, and counter-terrorism across Eurasian nations.

Why it exists

Eurasian nations face shared trans-border challenges including ethnic separatism, religious extremism, narcotics trafficking, and regional transit infrastructure bottlenecks.

How it works

Heads of state gather at annual multilateral summits to establish policy frameworks, supported by permanent secretariats and dedicated regional anti-terrorist bodies.

Fixed facts

  • Marks 25 years of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (founded 2001); 10 members (6 founders China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan; India and Pakistan 2017; Iran 2023; Belarus 2024)
  • New institutions: Universal Centre (Tashkent), Anti-Drug Centre (Dushanbe).

Traps

The organisation focuses on Eurasian regional stability and economic corridors rather than forming a mutual collective defense treaty alliance.

In the news

  • SCO: 25th Council of Heads of Government, Dushanbe
  • 26th SCO Summit, Bishkek (Kyrgyzstan)
★ BIMSTEC regional cooperation

In plain words: A regional sub-continental organization fosters economic connectivity, maritime transit, and technological cooperation among littoral and adjacent Bay of Bengal nations.

Why it exists

South Asian and Southeast Asian neighbours required a cooperative bridge to unlock maritime logistics, regional power trade, and counter-terrorism collaboration.

How it works

Member states cooperate across prioritized sectors including transport, trade, counter-terrorism, and energy grids through ministerial meetings and a permanent secretariat.

Fixed facts

  • BIMSTEC = Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation; 7 members (Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand); Secretariat Dhaka.
  • Evolution: BIST-EC (1997) → BIMST-EC with Myanmar (1998) → BIMSTEC (2004).

Traps

The group links South Asia with Southeast Asia through the Bay of Bengal littoral rather than operating within the continental framework of SAARC.

In the news

  • First BIMSTEC Home Ministers' Meeting, New Delhi
★ Indian Ocean Rim Association

In plain words: A regional maritime organization promotes sustainable ocean economic growth, maritime security, trade facilitation, and disaster management across rim nations.

Why it exists

The Indian Ocean encompasses vital international trade lanes requiring collective coordination against piracy, marine environmental degradation, and natural disasters.

How it works

Foreign ministers convene periodically to establish collaborative maritime projects, supported by working groups on maritime safety, blue economy, and academic cooperation.

  • Participants named: Iran, UAE, Oman, Yemen, South Africa, Mauritius, Indonesia

The association makes decisions strictly by consensus and excludes bilateral disputes and security alliances from its formal multilateral agenda.

★ UN High Seas Treaty (BBNJ)

In plain words: A global ocean conservation agreement establishes binding legal frameworks to protect marine biodiversity and govern international waters beyond territorial boundaries.

Why it exists

Oceans outside national exclusive economic zones faced unchecked commercial exploitation, illegal fishing, and habitat destruction without comprehensive governance.

How it works

Signatory nations establish marine protected sanctuaries, mandate rigorous environmental impact assessments for oceanic activities, and share benefits from marine genetic resources.

Fixed facts

  • Four areas: marine genetic resources and benefit sharing, area-based tools, environmental impact assessments, capacity building
  • India has signed but not ratified

Traps

The treaty applies exclusively to waters beyond national jurisdiction and does not compromise sovereign coastal rights within exclusive economic zones.

In the news

  • UN High Seas treaty enters into force: 17 Jan 2026
★ Global Hunger Index methodology

In plain words: An annual global report benchmarks national hunger levels across countries using multi-dimensional indicators of child malnutrition and undernourishment.

Why it exists

Monitoring child survival, dietary adequacy, and chronic undernourishment enables governments to assess the severity of nutritional deprivations across regions.

How it works

Researchers calculate composite scores from four standardized indicators of child stunting, child wasting, child mortality, and caloric undernourishment.

Fixed facts

  • India 100th of 122 countries, score 24.1 = ‘serious’ (tied with Ethiopia); in the serious category since 2012; score 25.8 in 2018.
  • Bands: ≤9.9 low; 10–19.9 moderate; 20–34.9 serious; 35–49.9 alarming; 50+ extremely alarming.

Traps

The index measures multidimensional food vulnerability and child growth failure rather than relying solely on agricultural cereal crop production.

In the news

  • GHI 2026
★ Global Gender Gap Index benchmarking

In plain words: An international index measures gender-based disparities across national economies to track parity progress between women and men over time.

Why it exists

Persistent gender imbalances in economic participation, political voice, and educational attainment hinder economic productivity and human welfare.

How it works

Researchers score national performance across economic opportunities, educational outcomes, health indicators, and political representation on a zero-to-one parity scale.

Fixed facts

  • World Economic Forum, 20th edition, 145 economies; 4 dimensions: economic participation, education, health & survival, political empowerment
  • In South Asia India trails Bangladesh (79th), Maldives (119th), Nepal (120th), Bhutan (121st), Sri Lanka (129th)

Traps

The index measures the relative gap between men and women within each nation rather than absolute levels of economic wealth or human development.

In the news

  • Global Gender Gap Index 2026: India 131st
★ World Migration Report and remittance flows

In plain words: A biennial global report analyzes international migration patterns, migrant demographics, and global diaspora financial remittance corridors.

Why it exists

International worker mobility shapes global economic development, generating essential family remittance transfers that support developing economies.

How it works

The international migration agency compiles global border transit statistics, labour migration flows, and commercial banking remittance records from central banks.

Fixed facts

  • India received $137.67 bn in 2024: world's top receiver and only country above $100 bn; top since 2010 ($53.48 bn then)
  • International migrants 304 million in 2024 (3.7% of world population; 2.9% in 1990); global remittances $905 bn

Traps

Remittance data captures formal personal financial transfers transmitted across official channels, excluding merchandise goods or unofficial informal money transfers.

In the news

  • World Migration Report 2026: India top remittance receiver
★ Sample Registration System and NCRB crime statistics

In plain words: Official government demographic surveys and statistical records publish national fertility rates, infant mortality indicators, and annual criminal offense statistics.

Why it exists

Reliable demographic tracking and centralized crime recording guide national healthcare resource allocation, policing reforms, and social welfare programs.

How it works

Sample demographic units record vital births and deaths across states, while police headquarters compile statutory criminal cases under standardized categories.

Fixed facts

  • SRS 2024 (Registrar General of India, MHA): TFR 1.9 (below replacement 2.1 for the fifth year; rural 2.1, urban 1.5); crude death rate 6.4; IMR 25 → 24 (Kerala lowest, 8); under-five mortality 28; sex ratio at birth 918 (2022-24)
  • NCRB set up 1986 (Tandon Committee, National Police Commission, MHA task force 1985)

Traps

Reported crime statistics reflect official police crime registration rates rather than representing true total offenses occurring within communities.

In the news

  • SRS Statistical Report 2024 and NCRB Crime in India 2024
