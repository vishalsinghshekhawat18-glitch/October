★ Integrated GST (IGST) on gold, silver, platinum imports
In plain words: Banks and approved agencies that import gold, silver and platinum now pay a small import tax on them. This was done because the earlier exemption ended, which puts these imports on the same footing as other ways of bringing in the metals.
  • Imports by banks and nominated agencies: 3% IGST from 1 Apr 2026.
  • Why: exemption list not extended beyond 31 Mar 2026 → tax parity with other import routes, including bullion.

★ India–Mauritius tax treaty amended: Principal Purpose Test (PPT)
In plain words: India changed its tax treaty with Mauritius so that people cannot use Mauritius just to avoid paying tax. The treaty exists so that income is not taxed twice, and the new test stops it from being misused.

★ 57th GST Council meeting (8 Oct 2026)
In plain words: The council that decides goods and services tax rules made the system gentler and simpler. It removed the power to arrest for certain offences, reduced some penalties, and moved refunds towards being handled automatically by the system.
  • Who: GST Council (chair Nirmala Sitharaman), Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi. What: process reforms + decriminalisation. Affects: all taxpayers.

  • Optional Annual Return Quarterly Payment (ARQP) for B2C-only taxpayers up to ₹5 cr turnover; e-invoicing extended to reverse-charge supplies from unregistered persons and import of services (turnover ₹5 cr+); GSTAT aligned with Tribunals Reforms Act, 2026.

★ GST registration for small e-commerce sellers from 1 November; new RCM e-invoice categories
In plain words: Small online sellers can get a simpler GST registration. They can sell in other states through the e-commerce platform's warehouse without opening their own shop or office there.

Foreign exchange rules

★ New FEMA rules for freelancers and influencers
In plain words: New rules now cover people in India who earn money from foreign clients, such as freelancers and online creators. They must record these earnings with their bank and bring the money into India within a fixed time.
  • Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026; effective 1 Oct 2026.
  • Change: export of services now under the Export Declaration Form (EDF) via authorised dealer (AD) banks (earlier only goods and software exporters).
  • Declare full value of services exported; file within 30 days from end of the month in which invoices are raised.
  • Realisation and repatriation: foreign-currency invoice → 9 months; rupee invoice → 12 months.
  • Invoices up to ₹10 lakh: self-declaration to bank is enough to close record, even if client pays less.
  • Unpaid > 1 year past due date → further foreign work only against full advance payment.
  • Remember: EDF = reporting/compliance, not a new tax.
  • Rules supersede the 2015 regulations: one unified EDF for goods, services and software. Realisation period cut: 15 → 9 months (foreign currency), 18 → 12 months (rupee). For goods via EDI ports, EDF is deemed filed with the shipping bill. New Regulation 20: ADs can handle pre-1 Oct 2026 cases that needed RBI approval.

★ CRR daily maintenance raised
In plain words: Banks must keep a fixed part of their deposits with the Reserve Bank of India as cash. The rule now forces them to hold almost the full required amount every single day, which leaves them less room to manage their cash and can make borrowing costlier.

★ Global Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) 2026 (UNDP + OPHI)
In plain words: A United Nations report measures poverty not just by income but by health, education and living conditions. It shows that India has cut such poverty sharply, while the number of poor people in the world rose for the first time.
  • Three dimensions: Health, Education, Standard of Living; scale 0–1 (higher = poorer).
  • Coverage: 108 countries, 6.3 bn people; 1.2 bn in acute multidimensional poverty (18.9%); 617 mn are children; 83% rural.

  • India: MPI 0.283 → 0.069 (2005/06 → 2019/21, 14.5 years); incidence 55.1% → 16.4%.
  • Static: SDG target 1.2 = halve poverty in all dimensions by 2030.

★ UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2026: The Point of Return
In plain words: A United Nations environment report says the world can no longer realistically keep warming within the safest limit, and that countries must cut pollution quickly. It shows how far present policies fall short.
  • 1.5°C with no/limited overshoot is no longer achievable; return to 1.5°C by 2100 still possible via deep cuts + CO₂ removal (about 320 Gt CO₂ to remove and store).
  • Warming by 2100: current policies 2.8°C; commitments met 2.6°C; plus net-zero pledges 2.1°C.
  • Needed cuts vs baseline: 18% by 2030, 45% by 2035, 69% by 2040; about 6% a year (2025–2035).
