☆ GDP concepts from the PIB explainer
  • Double deflation: output and inputs deflated separately; real GVA = real output − real intermediate consumption (production side; IMF-preferred)
  • If input prices rise faster than output prices the implicit GVA deflator can turn negative — not a fall in manufacturing prices
  • Implicit GDP deflator = (GDP at current prices ÷ GDP at constant prices) × 100; derived from 300+ item deflators, so need not track CPI/WPI
  • GDP discrepancy = balancing item between production and expenditure approaches; large nominal–real gap in mining can just reflect mineral price rises

★ Govt notifies zero charges on UPI transactions up to ₹2,000
In plain words: The government said small digital payments will stay free of charge. It keeps everyday payments cheap for people and shows how fees apply on larger shop payments.
  • Notified by Department of Financial Services (DFS), Ministry of Finance; legal basis Section 10A, Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007
  • UPI (Unified Payments Interface) transactions up to ₹2,000: no direct/indirect charges → keeps ≈ 96% of UPI volume zero-charge
  • Person-to-Person (P2P) UPI stays free
  • P2M (Person-to-Merchant) above ₹2,000 → MDR 0.4%; for ₹75,000 and above, capped at ₹300 per transaction
  • MDR (Merchant Discount Rate) = fee a merchant pays to accept digital payments
  • All RuPay debit card payments exempt from charges; no blanket MDR on merchants
  • Flat ₹5 MDR for transactions above ₹2,000 in railways, telecom, insurance, fuel, agri inputs; capital markets (MFs, brokers) 0.02% capped ₹300; small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh/month by UPI QR keep zero MDR
  • MDR applies to only ≈4% of merchant transactions; 5% of MDR collections go to a small-merchant UPI fund; MDR is not a government tax
  • MDR on UPI payments above ₹2,000 attracts 18% GST
  • UPI & Services Steering Committee (22 members) examining MDR: led by NPCI CEO Dilip Asbe and Executive Director Sohini Rajola
Exam angle: Up to what amount are UPI transactions made free of charge, and what is the MDR above it?
Remember: Free ≤ ₹2,000; MDR 0.4%; cap ₹300 from ₹75,000.
